## CL-P1 — Sell-absorption share at E (SOL-weighted; the kind of sell statistic R83(d) asks for)
Hypothesis: on an active tape, the share of curve SOL leaving in the last 60 s before the 5-min decision (sell SOL ÷ buy SOL, received rows only, evaluator dedup, curve only) separates launches whose demand is being met by early sellers from launches whose demand is still net new; a high share precedes worse after-cost exits at the E entry. Why it might work: CL-L011 showed a raw "no sell" count binds on inactivity (CK21); a SOL-weighted share is defined on every active tape and measures the same thing without the inactivity confound. How to test: statistic s = Σ sell SOL / Σ buy SOL over received curve rows in (created + 240 s, created + 300 s]; veto when s ≥ 0.5 (threshold from the prior that sells matching half the inflow is the point at which the trail exit tends to trigger within a minute of entry on CK16's kind of tape); pre-check the bind share first; report kept / removed / paired as in F012. Knowable-at-time: all rows have recv_ms ≤ decision. Distinct from CL-L001 (an entry after one ≥ 1 SOL sell is absorbed) and from CL-L004 (an exit rule).

## CL-P2 — Buy-size escalation at E
Hypothesis: when the last ten outside curve buys before the decision are increasing in SOL (Spearman rank correlation of buy SOL against receipt order ≥ +0.5), the tape is being bid up by growing conviction rather than by a fixed-size bot ladder, and the E entry does better after costs; when they are decreasing, worse. Why it might work: CL-L002 found most-common-size dominance does not separate outcomes; a trend statistic asks a different question (direction, not diversity) and is invisible to a "fill the tape with 0.1 SOL buys" farm. How to test: last 10 received non-creator curve buys by created + 300 s (require ≥ 10 — minimum-n pre-check beside the bind share, as R86 (3) requires for count-dependent statistics); rank correlation; registered direction: skip when ρ ≤ −0.5 (falling sizes). One threshold, fixed first.

## CL-P3 — Creation-clock alignment (unconventional)
Hypothesis: launches whose creation time sits within ± 2 s of a whole minute (or of a whole 30 s) are over-represented among scheduled / scripted launches, and scripted launches trade worse at E after costs than hand-launched ones. Why it might work: the creation timestamp is the one feature that is known before any trade, costs nothing, and is never gamed by a tape-filler because no one optimises the second of the minute; a cron-driven launcher produces a spike at :00 that a human population does not. How to test: counts only first — the distribution of (create_ts mod 60) over the census and over E, with a χ² against uniform (a descriptive fact, not a result); if the :00 ± 2 s bin is over-represented (pre-check: bind share within 5–95 % on E), register "skip E launches with create_ts mod 60 ∈ [58, 2]" and score once on a released window. Knowable-at-time trivially. Distinct from the creation-metadata veto R80 declined (name / symbol features) and from creator-cadence (R60) — it conditions on the clock, not on the creator's history. The tracker's create_ts source (receipt vs chain block time) must be stated in the PROTOCOL; if it is receipt time, the statistic measures the tracker's clock, and the proposal is withdrawn.
