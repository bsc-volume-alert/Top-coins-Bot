## 2. Step 2 — what is and is not knowable at the decision time in the study (statements, then the numbers that quantify them)
(a) Chain time, not receipt. The study triggers and lands on `ts` (block time). The tracker sees a trade at `recv_ms`. On the 307 RUN2 launches entered by both the study's logic and the knowable-at-time (KAT) spec, the KAT decision comes a median 0.77 s later (p90 9.0 s; RUN3 0.79 / 13.3 s; RUN5 0.91 / 29.3 s); the median receipt lag of the trigger trade itself is 0.79–0.80 s. Small on the median, long-tailed.
(b) The population screen "≥ 20 trades and tape ≥ 600 s" is evaluated on the whole tape, so at a 60-s (or 195-s) decision it is a condition on the future. On the 3,638-launch denominator 2,042 launches fail it. Under the KAT BASE60 entry those 2,042 are the worst trades: 1,665 attempted / 664 resolved / −40.48 SOL (−0.061 per trade, 26.7 % lose > 50 %, 60 % unknown) against 1,275 / 963 / −19.38 (−0.020, 15.8 %, 24 % unknown) on the 1,596 that pass. The screen removes two thirds of the 60-s loss by looking ahead; the same screen sits under every trigger's denominator.
(c) "Unresolved" positions are valued at the last printed price × 0.985 and counted. Under the register's rule they are unknown, not a number. KAT unknown shares: BASE60 45 % of attempts (804 exits land in a logged capture gap, 231 on a state older than 30 s, 259 fills stale), RUN2 13 %, RUN3 16 %, RUN5 22 %.
(d) Costs: 1.5 % flat each way on the next printed trade's price; no reserve-based impact, no 0.0015 SOL fixed fee, no 30-s staleness limit. R4 applies 125 bps curve / 55 bps pool per leg on the recorded reserves plus the fixed fee.
(e) Population "at least one paper book bought it" is a launcher-side selection not derivable from the allowlisted files; the 782 window mints are used as given and the full retained denominator (3,638) is reported beside them. Whether a book's 60-s bar is knowable is not in question (it is); whether the book population is the right denominator is.
(f) 2-h horizon on tapes that stop at ~125 min of retention or earlier; a "horizon" exit at px[-1] where the tape ended early is unknown.
(g) GRAD and pool rows: pool capture before tracker 0.19.8 / 0.19.9 is partial and pool states lack evidence fields; eval-1.1 treats those fills as unavailable. Not re-scored here (GRAD is a different mechanism; left for a registered task if wanted).
(h) t0 = first trade's chain time, not the census create_ts; p60 is the price at t0 + 60 s. KAT uses created + 60 s on received trades.
Knowable at the time, and kept: the trigger prices (a received trade ≥ k × the price at 60 s), the 60–900 s window, the trail exit on sellable proceeds.

## 4. Verdict on F004
The study reproduces exactly from raw allowlisted data on the allowlisted subset (2,171/2,171 rows, every field). Its positive run-entry totals rest on four things that are not knowable or not allowed at the decision time: a tape-length screen on the future (removes the worst two thirds of the 60-s loss), unknowns scored as the last print, flat 1.5 % costs without impact or staleness, and a book-selected population; receipt timing is a small median effect with a long tail. Re-scored knowable-at-time under R4 and eval-1.1 on every retained launch, BASE60, RUN2, RUN3 and RUN5 are all losses (−59.86 / −16.34 / −4.38 / −2.47 SOL), losses without their best trades, with per-trade differences to the 60-s entry that straddle zero and no reduction in the share of > 50 % losses. Not profitable; nothing supported; no parameter was tuned (k and the window are the study's own). The study's qualitative reading — "a lottery at roughly a fair price, the whole profit is a few coins" — is the generous end of what the allowlisted window supports; the register's reading is "a loss whose size shrinks with later entry because fewer trades are taken".
